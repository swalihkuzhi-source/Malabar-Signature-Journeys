H1: Malabar Plays Football Like Nowhere Else
Picture a floodlit ground in a Malabar village, packed from the touchline to the treetops, with thousands of voices rising as seven players a side chase a single ball. This is Sevens, a faster, tighter and fiercer version of football, and from November to May it fills grounds across Malappuram, Calicut, Kannur, Wayanad and Kasaragod. Malappuram is its heart, and Calicut is the city where Malabar’s football faith grew up. Sevens began on post-harvest paddy fields, where villagers kicked a ball after the crops were cut, and it grew into a season of tournaments run by local clubs, often raising money for charity, with African players flying in to join the local stars. Watching it is not like watching a match. It is like joining a festival.
With Malabar Ways you go in with a local fan host, who walks you to the best safe seat, explains the clubs and the chants, and shares match-night snacks and tea. A dance of the arabana, a small single-sided flat hand drum, played to welcome guests and honour heroes. The drummers sway, toss the drums overhead and catch them, all in step. Its cousin, Duff Muttu, uses the larger round duff.
Where: Kannur, Kasaragod, Calicut | Book this experience
Kolkali (https://share.google/gRSVRw83xTSZT5Y3W)
When they click open to this part below
Dancers move in shifting circles, striking decorated sticks together in rising rhythm. The Mappila version opens with prayer and carries Islamic songs, and it is rooted in the stepwork of Kalarippayattu.
Where: Kannur, Calicut, Malappuram | Book this experience
Paricha Muttu (https://share.google/a5mTUdDOB8WNLeXIv)
When they click open to this part below
A martial art turned performance. Twelve men with shields (paricha) and swords clash in rhythm to steps taken from Kalarippayattu, led by the master in the centre and opened with prayers and Arabi-Malayalam songs.
Where: Calicut, Malappuram | Book this experience
Vattappattu (https://share.google/8ONzFDDTuiTRtIm9S)
When they click open to this part below
A circle of singers around the bride on a wedding night. Groups of men and women answer each other, song for song, with handclaps and a brass spittoon beaten with an areca leaf fan, in a friendly contest that can run until dawn.
Where: Malappuram, Calicut | Book this experience
Muttippattu (https://share.google/rXalV979cH6WN5LBB)
When they click open to this part below

The clap-led song tradition of Mappila gatherings, closely tied to the Kaimuttippattu singing of weddings. Voices and handclaps carry the rhythm, with no need for instruments.
Where: Malappuram, Calicut, Kannur | Book this experience

Cheeni Muttu and Kuthu Ratib (https://share.google/P3ty1zPMBRI1aBor1)
When they click open to this part below

Two devotional traditions, introduced with care. Cheeni Muttu is a festival music of the cheeni, a shahnai brought from Persia and played with drums at the Urs of saints, famously at Kondotty. Kuthu Ratib is a Sufi ritual of Arabic litanies and arabana drumming in praise of Shaikh Ahmad Rifai, which includes acts of piercing performed in devotion. Guests visit only by invitation, to listen and learn the meaning, never to take part.
Where: Malappuram (Kondotty), Calicut, Kannur | Cheeni Muttu: Book this experience | Kuthu Ratib: Request an introduction
Thira (https://share.google/xfQEalb8f2w4Uw96r)
When they click open to this part below 

A ritual dance-drama of the sacred groves of South Malabar, mainly in Kozhikode and Malappuram. Performers in coconut-leaf skirts, brass headgear and painted faces are believed to carry a deity, told across a night of drumming and song. It is the hereditary right of communities such as the Vannan and Peruvannan.
Where: Calicut, Malappuram, Wayanad, Kannur | Seasonal | Book this experience
Poorakkali (https://share.google/ykDtOtAFOi7MxzJIQ)
When they click open to this part below

A dance of the north Malabar Bhagavathy temples, in which men circle a lamp in rhythm, using steps drawn from Kalarippayattu. It is performed in the month of Meenam, around March and April.
Where: Kannur, Kasaragod | Seasonal | Book this experience

Wear a Little Art, Take Home a Beautiful Memory
Discover the beauty of henna, a natural art form cherished in Malabar’s wedding and festive traditions.
